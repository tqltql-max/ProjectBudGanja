Arbusto nativo (Baccharis trimera) — chás amargos do Sul/Sudeste; flavonoides e diterpenos em tradição digestiva popular.

Cuidados reforçados

Summary

Native shrub (Baccharis trimera) — bitter teas of southern/southeastern Brazil; flavonoids and diterpenes in popular digestive tradition.

Parts used

  • Aerial parts

Traditional uses (catalog)

  • Traditional bitter digestive teas
  • Popular hepatic/digestive herbal use (cultural context)
  • Regional decoctions

Cautions

Intense bitterness; prolonged use and pregnancy need guidance. Does not replace assessment of liver disease. Educational content — avoid self-medication.

Chemistry and markers

MarkerNotes
Flavonoids (e.g. quercetin, rutin)Dominant phenolic markers in aerial parts — antioxidant and hepatoprotective interest (animal models).
Chlorogenic acid and derivativesAbundant phenolic acid — common in native bitter plants.
Diterpenes and triterpenesLipophilic profile in ethanolic extracts — contributes to bitterness and documented bioactivity.
Condensed tanninsAstringent effect — relevant in concentrated decoctions.

Limits of chemical readout: profiles vary by cultivar, plant part, drying and preparation. This is not a clinical protocol or product assay.

Uses and benefits (educational context)

ThemeHow the lab frames it
Bitter digestive teaGaúcho and Southeast tradition — «cleanse» the liver (popular metaphor, not clinical).
Glycemic control (interest)Preliminary studies with extracts — do not replace diabetes treatment.
Mild diureticFolk use for fluid retention — monitor electrolytes with prolonged use.

Limits of evidence: folk tradition and scientific interest do not replace diagnosis, prescription or professional care.

Reinforced cautions

Hepatotoxicity reported with excessive or non-standardized use. Avoid in pregnancy, nursing and liver disease without supervision. Do not combine with other hepatotoxins.
